# Five seasons, restated under 2026/27 scoring

_Generated by `scripts/history_study.py`. Every number here is measured from the data in this repo; nothing is a ranking and nothing is a recommendation._

## Is the reconstruction trustworthy?

Rebuilt **action counts** against FPL's own, 339 players with 900+ minutes in 2025/26: r = **0.9949**, mean error +3.0 actions over a whole season (MAE 3.4).

Rebuilt **DefCon points** against the points actually awarded: r = **0.960**. Uncalibrated the league total comes out at 2043 against 2698 (-24.3%).

It runs **low**, not high, and for a reason worth stating: a fixed-rate Poisson under-counts threshold crossings. A real player's actions are overdispersed — some matches he chases the game for ninety minutes, some he is three up at half time — and P(actions ≥ threshold) is convex in the rate, so spreading the rate out raises the average. Left uncorrected it would understate every defender in the seasons before the rule existed and make this whole comparison say the opposite of the truth.

So the modelled points are scaled per position to the ones 2025/26 actually awarded (DEF ×1.26, FWD ×2.25, MID ×1.51), which brings the total to 2776 against 2698. The same correction is worth making in the projection model, where DefCon is computed the same way and is therefore also low.

## What this says about the projection model

**The model understates DefCon.** `xpts_calc.defcon_hit` assumes a player's defensive actions arrive at a constant rate, so it uses a Poisson. They do not: measured within player, across starts in 2025/26, the variance-to-mean ratio of per-match DefCon actions is **1.40 for defenders and 1.41 for midfielders**, against 1.00 for a Poisson. Because P(actions ≥ threshold) is convex in the rate, that overdispersion means more threshold crossings than a Poisson predicts, not fewer.

Scaled against what 2025/26 actually awarded, the shortfall is **DEF +26%**, **MID +51%**. At 2 points a match that is a material amount of a defender's season, and it lands on exactly the players a DefCon-heavy ruleset is supposed to reward. The fix is a negative binomial in place of the Poisson, with the dispersion fitted from the same per-match counts. **Not applied** — it changes where players sit relative to each other, so it is your call, not mine.

(The forward factor is left out of the sentence above because it rests on 18 DefCon points across four players in the whole league. It is noise, and it does not matter.)
